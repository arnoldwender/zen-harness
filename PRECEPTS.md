# The First Word

> The first utterance of the Zen Harness: a fixed line — one stroke of presence before the
> work — then a rotating *line of the day*, a classical haiku from the masters in W. G. Aston's
> public-domain translation (*A History of Japanese Literature*, 1899). Every line verified for
> wording and public-domain status.

## The fixed line

```text
One stroke, unhurried. Do this one thing completely.
```

## The rotating line

Beneath the fixed line, the harness prints one rotating haiku from [`precepts.txt`](precepts.txt)
— a line of the day, changing daily. Edit [`precepts.txt`](precepts.txt) (one `Line — Source`
per line) to curate or extend; keep only verified public-domain verse. The current pool:

> - Of Miïdera / The gate I would knock at— / The moon of to-day. — Bashō (1644–1694), tr. W. G. Aston, A History of Japanese Literature (1899)
> - I come weary, / In search of an inn— / Ah! these wistaria flowers! — Bashō (1644–1694), tr. W. G. Aston, A History of Japanese Literature (1899)
